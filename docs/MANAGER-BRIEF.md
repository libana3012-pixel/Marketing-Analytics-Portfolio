# Marketing performance | One-page decision brief

**Practice brief — fictional February–March 2026 examples.** This document illustrates communication style and does not claim real employer findings.

## Executive view
- The campaign sample records more clicks and conversions in March, with spend increasing from 1,800 to 1,970 CU.
- Cost per recorded conversion declines from 11.25 to 11.01 CU, while the example ROAS increases from 4.78 to 4.88.
- The organic-search sample also shows a higher total click count, but it is a **separate data source and should not be combined or attributed to campaign spend**.
- A website measurement plan proposes events for actions such as viewing directions or ticket information. These are proposed events, not deployed tracking or verified sales.

## Questions I would bring to a review meeting
1. Are conversions defined consistently across periods, and are they deduplicated?
2. Is the revenue in the report attributed or incremental? Do we have margin and refunds?
3. Did branded/non-branded mix, device mix or budget allocation change?
4. Are reported trends driven by volume, conversion rates or audience composition?
5. What needs to be instrumented before next month's reporting can be trusted?

## Recommended follow-up, not a claim of past action
Document conversion definitions and attribution windows, validate GA4 events in the actual environment before using them in decisions, then expand the reporting window and compare channel-level performance.

**Bottom line:** A good junior analyst is useful when they ask the right questions, make the numbers auditable, and avoid overclaiming.
